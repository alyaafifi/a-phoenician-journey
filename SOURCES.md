# Project source register

Add new references here under the relevant topic. For each one, record the
author or institution, the object or publication, the relevant pages or
sections when available, what the project uses it for, and any limits on
what it establishes. For visual assets, include their origin and license or
permission status when known.

The working documents carry the design decisions based on these sources.

## Writing, alphabet, and translation

| Source | Material covered and use | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Unicode Consortium, Core Specification 16.0, §10.3](https://unicode.org/versions/Unicode16.0.0/core-spec/chapter-10/) | Phoenician repertoire U+10900–U+1091F. The alphabet reference follows the 22 letter code points. The minigame uses right-to-left order and the popup explains repeated number signs. | A modern encoding reference covering several related historical forms. Use epigraphic evidence for the appearance and wording of any specific inscription. |
| [Metropolitan Museum of Art, *Alphabet Origins: From Kipling to Sinai*](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2014/assyria-to-iberia/blog/posts/alphabet) and [*Assyria to Iberia*, p. 46](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/Assyria_to_Iberia_at_the_Dawn_of_the_Classical_Age.pdf) | The popup's ox-head fact follows the interpretation of an early sign as the ancestor of Phoenician *alf*. The catalogue explains the 22 consonantal letters and how Greek writers adapted some letters for vowels, including the development illustrated by A. | The ox-head is an ancestral sign. The playable Phoenician glyph uses a later letterform. |
| [DEAPS, Ahiram sarcophagus, KAI 001](https://deaps.osu.edu/text_objects/11441) | Edited inscription and translations. Attests *GBL*, Byblos. | Funerary inscription with debated dating. The cargo letter is fictional. |
| [DEAPS, Abibaal inscription, KAI 005](https://deaps.osu.edu/text_objects/11445) | Edited inscription on an Egyptian object found at Byblos. Supports the proposed reading *MṢRM* for Egypt. | The edition discusses an alternative reading and reconstruction of damaged text. |
| [DEAPS, KAI 033](https://deaps.osu.edu/text_objects/11473) | Inscription naming Kition and Idalion. Source for the minigame's *KTY* spelling. | Evidence for the place name in this inscription. |
| [DEAPS, Saqqara papyrus, KAI 050](https://deaps.osu.edu/text_objects/11490) | Surviving Phoenician letter from Egypt. Its payment and receipt language and writing material inform the game's scribe and letter. | Different context and date from the fictional order. |
| [Charles R. Krahmalkov, *Phoenician-Punic Dictionary*](https://digitalthought.info/Phoenician-Punic-Dictionary.pdf) | Entries *SWT/SWYT* for garment or cloth and *MLḤM* for a proposed joiner or carpenter, citing RES 1319. Used for the letter and glossary. | Entries draw on texts across periods and dialects. The full cargo wording needs specialist review. |
| [Noto Sans Phoenician](https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/notosansphoenician) | The font used for the parchment and alphabet key. [SIL Open Font License](unity/Assets/Resources/Harbor2D/NotoSansPhoenician-OFL.txt). | Font shapes are modern renderings of the encoded script. |

## Byblos and the waterfront

| Source | Material covered and use | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [UNESCO, Byblos World Heritage listing](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/295/) and [property documentation](https://whc.unesco.org/document/4621) | Site geography, long occupation, and historical timber trade. Grounds the promontory setting and the choice of cedar. | The game's precise harbor layout and the use of cedar in a particular repair are reconstructed. |
| [Francis-Allouche and colleagues, Byblos maritime field report](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/Levant/Byblos-FrancisAllouche2017.pdf) | Survey, sediments, and maps. Figures 2, 19–20 and 29 inform El-Yasmine and the southern bay. Pages 204–207 and 224–228 discuss the buried basin and dating. | Better-defined basin sediments include Persian and Hellenistic phases. Exact Iron Age shoreline and quay geometry remain uncertain. |
| [Charaf and Francis-Allouche, 2026 maritime synthesis](https://anetoday.org/byblos-maritime-hub/) and [2025 monograph summary, AIBL](https://aibl.fr/collections/tome-65-byblos-et-la-mer-les-abords-maritimes-et-le-systeme-portuaire-antique/) | Maps, imagery, and project synthesis for the southern maritime approach, headland and offshore islet. | Supports relative placement. The game's modeled height and shoreline are artistic choices. |
| [Honor Frost Foundation, Path to Harbor, Mission 11](https://honorfrostfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Byblos-and-the-Sea-Summary-Report-Path-to-Harbor-Mission-11.pdf) | Excavations on the southern approach and its relationship to the settlement and water. | The excavated gate is Middle Bronze Age. |
| [Noureddine, Byblos stone anchor survey](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/ETUDESarchivees/Navires/Anchors/AnchorsByblos-Noureddine2016.pdf) and [Israel Antiquities Authority, Atlit underwater excavation](https://hadashot.iaa.org.il/report_detail_eng.aspx?id=132&mag_id=110) | Local examples of stone anchors and a possible mooring weight. Atlit is the regional comparison for a stone quay. | The Atlit quay provides a comparison for construction, with no direct evidence for the game's quay at Byblos. |

## Crafts, ships, and trade

| Source | Material covered and use | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Smithsonian Museum Conservation Institute, Sarepta residue analysis](https://mci.si.edu/node/1283986) | Chemical identification of purple residue in a 13th-century BCE context. Supports the dye craft. | Earlier and at another site. |
| [Tel Shiqmona workshop study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0321082) ([open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12002455/)) | Iron Age vat and workshop evidence, used for the dye stall's vessels and trade context. | The Byblos stall layout and playable dip sequence are designed for the game. |
| [Timna royal-purple textile study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0245897) | Dye analysis and discussion of Hebrew and Akkadian purple terms. Informs the proposed cognate in the draft letter. | Linguistic comparison. The Phoenician wording remains provisional. |
| [Iron Age transport jar research](https://open.rstfen.cnr.it/index.php/rsf/article/download/180/103/788) | Typology and drawings for vessel shoulders, handles and bases. Art reference for pottery and cargo jars. | The gameplay steps and individual jar at Byblos are reconstructions. |
| [Tell el-Burak wine-press excavation and plaster analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/phoenician-lime-for-phoenician-wine-iron-age-plaster-from-a-wine-press-at-tell-elburak-lebanon/2C1D1FFA859C0D9A32E79447D35F9FF6) | Treading area, channel, collecting vat, and plaster. Grounds the proposed wine-press layout. | Site-specific installation. Gameplay compresses production time. |
| [ARQUA, Mazarrón 2](https://www.cultura.gob.es/mnarqua/en/colecciones/piezas-seleccionadas/fenicio/barco-2.html) and [Murcia interpretation centre](https://www.turismoregiondemurcia.es/en/interpretation_centre/centro-de-interpretacion-del-barco-fenicio-399/) | Late seventh-century BCE hull, basket, rope and packing material. Vessel dimensions inform the working-boat model. | Wreck from Spain. The prototype hull, its timber, and the layout of the hold are reconstructed. |
| [Ballard et al., Ashkelon wrecks, *AJA* 106.2](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.2307/4126241), [WHOI field project](https://www.whoi.edu/sbl/printPage.do?aid=5018&litesiteid=2740), and [OXREP, Elissa](https://oxrep.classics.ox.ac.uk/databases/shipwrecks_database/620to640/records/1746/) | Eighth-century BCE wrecks Tanit and Elissa. Concentrated jars, interpreted as wine cargo, inform cargo quantity and stowage design. | Egypt and Carthage are possible destinations. The game's Byblos departure, route and exact jar bay are fictional. |
| [Metropolitan Museum of Art, *Phoenician Sailing*](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2014/assyria-to-iberia/blog/posts/phoenician-sailing) | Ancient ship imagery for the round-hulled merchant silhouette. | The game's detailed rigging and paint are artistic choices. |
| [RINAP, Tiglath-pileser III 32](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/rinap/Q003445) and [Livius, Kition](https://www.livius.org/articles/place/kition/) | Assyrian tribute list including Byblos and dyed textiles, alongside background on Phoenician settlement at Kition. | The tribute traveled overland. The Byblos-to-Kition cloth shipment and dry locker are gameplay reconstructions. |
