# Phoenician letter minigame

The letter is a modern reconstruction for the game. The Phoenician text in
`LetterScreen.cs` is a draft for specialist review. The English tiles give the
intended meaning of each line and the instructions the player needs.

- The Byblos and Egypt names draw on [KAI 001](https://deaps.osu.edu/text_objects/11441)
  and [KAI 005](https://deaps.osu.edu/text_objects/11445). The latter discusses
  the reading of Egypt in the Abibaal inscription. The spelling of Kition
  draws on [KAI 033](https://deaps.osu.edu/text_objects/11473).
- The garment and cloth vocabulary draws on the entry for *SWT* and its
  plural *SWYT* in [Krahmalkov's Phoenician-Punic Dictionary](https://digitalthought.info/Phoenician-Punic-Dictionary.pdf).
  The carpenter term *MLḤM* draws on the dictionary's reading of RES 1319
  as a joiner of boards.
  The purple term *ʾrgmn* is a proposed cognate based on Northwest Semitic
  vocabulary. The [Timna textile study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0245897)
  discusses the Hebrew and Akkadian parallels. The cedar and board terms
  are provisional cognate-based reconstructions.
- The script uses the [Unicode Phoenician repertoire](https://unicode.org/versions/Unicode16.0.0/core-spec/chapter-10/).
  The player reads each line right to left. Word spacing is provided as a
  reading aid. The game renders the glyphs with Noto Sans Phoenician,
  distributed with its SIL Open Font License in the same Resources folder.

Review the grammar, shipping vocabulary, Cyprus name, and foreman title
before presenting the text as a historical reconstruction. The fictional
order is for purple cloth and cedar boards. This change adds the
translation minigame. The ship still uses its existing cloth-and-wine order.
