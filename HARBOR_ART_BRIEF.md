# Byblos harbor · 3D art brief

**Setting** · Byblos (Jbeil, Lebanon), c. 800–600 BCE.

**Starting point** · [Sprint 1 Blender landing](art/sprint-1/harbor-landing.blend) and the [browser walk-through plan](SPRINT_1.md).

**Target** · A small working landing at the southern foot of the ancient town, with a rocky headland uphill, an open sandy bay, and El-Yasmine offshore.

## Historical basis

- **Documented at Byblos** · The town occupied a sandstone promontory. Survey, sediment cores, and a trial trench identify a buried marine basin at its southern foot, near the Qassouba valley. The wider El-Skhyné bay was exposed to the sea. Its northern corner near Jouret Osman had relative shelter from southwesterly winds behind El-Yasmine. The northern cove, where the modern fishing harbor sits, was shallow and rocky. Use the southern geography as the scene reference. [UNESCO site description](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/295/), [Byblos maritime field report, figs. 2, 19–20 and 29](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/Levant/Byblos-FrancisAllouche2017.pdf), [Charaf and Francis-Allouche, 2026 synthesis with maps and images](https://anetoday.org/byblos-maritime-hub/)
- **Date limits** · The former shoreline identified around 100 m inland of the modern coast is evidence for the buried basin. The better-defined C1 sediments are late Persian and Hellenistic. The excavated southern gate is Middle Bronze Age. Use these findings to understand the relationship between the town, valley and water. The exact Iron Age shoreline, depth, descent from the town, and quay design remain unresolved. [Byblos maritime field report, pp. 204–207 and 224–228](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/Levant/Byblos-FrancisAllouche2017.pdf), [Southern approach excavations](https://honorfrostfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Byblos-and-the-Sea-Summary-Report-Path-to-Harbor-Mission-11.pdf), [2025 monograph summary](https://aibl.fr/collections/tome-65-byblos-et-la-mer-les-abords-maritimes-et-le-systeme-portuaire-antique/)
- **Reconstruction for this game** · The sandy cargo landing, mooring arrangement, location of the dye stall and scribe, and individual boats are proposed designs. Published Byblos surveys identify stone anchors and a possible mooring weight. The 2026 field synthesis still treats quays as a subject for future investigation. A documented stone quay at Iron Age Atlit establishes regional capability, and should remain a comparison for any future masonry variant. [Byblos stone anchor survey](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/ETUDESarchivees/Navires/Anchors/AnchorsByblos-Noureddine2016.pdf), [Byblos maritime field report, pp. 226–228](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/Levant/Byblos-FrancisAllouche2017.pdf), [Atlit underwater excavation](https://hadashot.iaa.org.il/report_detail_eng.aspx?id=132&mag_id=110)

## Build the landscape in this order

1. **Check the starting landing in the browser.** Walk from the upper approach to the cargo area and test the ramp and station spacing before extending the terrain. Keep the 1.7 m reference marker, a clear route about 2.5–3 m wide, and the water at Blender Z = 0. Keep the existing source origin and orientation for the first art pass.
2. **Shape the bay in plan view.** Extend the straight test beach into an asymmetric curve. Put the landing in the relatively sheltered corner of the southern bay. Carry the beach away along the larger open curve. Let the Qassouba valley suggest a low inland opening, using a shallow gravelly depression or a dry wash at the margin of the scene. Keep an uninterrupted opening toward the Mediterranean. Treat the current walkable area as one piece of a larger coast, with the headland and outer shoreline extending past it in the view.
3. **Build the headland uphill.** This is the land projection the ancient town sits on. Start with two or three broad sandstone masses and a flatter top. Let the land rise behind and to one side of the landing so the player can read the coast and town in one glance. Use a worn earth path or a low rough-stone retaining edge to suggest the uphill connection. A small cluster of simple rooflines can imply the town if the skyline needs it. Keep the excavated Bronze Age gate and the Crusader castle out of the prototype.
4. **Place El-Yasmine offshore.** Build it as a low, broad rock silhouette, with exposed rock shelves at the water and an irregular top. Position it near the headland end of the southern bay, on the seaward side of the quieter inlet. Keep water visible between it and the mainland. Leave a wider opening to open sea on the exposed side. Its modeled shape, height, and distance are artistic choices. Use the field maps to judge relative placement. [Byblos maritime field report, figs. 2 and 20](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/Levant/Byblos-FrancisAllouche2017.pdf), [2026 synthesis and labeled reconstruction](https://anetoday.org/byblos-maritime-hub/)
5. **Dress from large shapes to small props.** Read the promontory, islet, and landing from the upper path before spending time on planks, shells, or rope. Then check from the cargo area at player eye height. From there the town rise should read landward, with the islet and open sea visible beyond the boat.

## Asset direction

Use a grounded, stylized low-poly look for this pass. Favor recognizable silhouettes, large material changes, and a few details at eye level. Use warm buff sandstone, muted tan sand and earth, grey-brown timber, straw-colored fibre, reddish terracotta, and blue-green water. Reserve the strongest purple for dyed textiles and the vat rim. Suggested dimensions and the palette are production choices.

| Component | Model and dress |
| --- | --- |
| **Headland and outcrops** | Use broad, angular sandstone shelves, broken edges, and a few shallow horizontal layers. Make the land mass continuous with the coast and let the path cut along the slope. Add scattered rubble where rock meets packed earth. |
| **Beach and water** | Use a shallow sandy ramp, a darker wet strip, and sparse rounded gravel around the wash and rock shelves. Keep the protected corner relatively smooth and use a little more wave texture toward the mouth. Keep the water surface level. If it crosses the boat interior in the current preview, address that when placing the finished hull and water together. |
| **Cargo landing** | Keep a level dry staging patch above the wet edge. Start with the existing footprint of roughly 5 × 4 m and retain a clear walk around the props. Use hand-carried jars and cloth bundles, a low stack of goods, rope, and a short loose plank only if it helps bridge the visual gap to the boat. Keep the shoreline mostly sand and exposed rock. A small pierced stone anchor can appear beside the gear as a Byblos-inspired prop. |
| **Dye stall** | Use a roughly 3 × 3 m shelter on dry ground, with timber poles and a woven reed or plain fabric shade. Place one broad ceramic vat where the player can see its stained inner rim, plus a shell basket and a hanging swatch. The stall layout is invented. Use the excavated vat shapes from Tel Shiqmona and treat Sarepta as earlier evidence for the craft. |
| **Scribe’s table** | Set a modest timber table and stool under shade between the uphill path and the cargo area. Use a small rolled sheet or laid-out sheet, a reed pen, and a small ink vessel. Keep lettering as a placeholder until the project wording is verified. |
| **Jars, cloth and baskets** | Choose a specific eighth- or seventh-century BCE transport-jar profile from the typology and trace its shoulder, neck, base, and two handles. Use matte terracotta with gentle variation. Fold or roll a few cloth bundles and tie them with plain cord. Use a woven fibre basket and rope around the staged cargo. Avoid modern pallets, barrels and standardized shipping crates. |

**Workshop and cargo references** · Shiqmona provides Iron Age workshop and vat evidence from c. 1100–600 BCE. Sarepta’s chemically identified purple residue is from a 13th-century BCE context. The Saqqara papyrus is a sixth-century BCE letter from Egypt and supplies a writing-material reference. The eighth-century BCE Ashkelon wrecks supply an example of jars shipped in quantity, interpreted as wine cargo. None of those finds fixes a workshop or cargo manifest at Byblos. [Shiqmona excavation and analyses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12002455/), [Sarepta residue analysis](https://mci.si.edu/node/1283986), [Saqqara letter, KAI 50](https://deaps.osu.edu/text_objects/11490), [Iron Age transport-jar typology and drawings](https://open.rstfen.cnr.it/index.php/rsf/article/download/180/103/788), [Ashkelon wreck survey](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.2307/4126241)

### Boats and ship

**Nearshore working boat.** Model one open timber hull with a broad middle and tapered ends. Build the hull silhouette first, then add an inner floor, a few visible frames or crosspieces, a plain gunwale, and one or two oars or lines. Set the hull into the water with the rim and interior cargo visible. Lay it roughly along the shore so the boat and staging area read as one working space. Use the late seventh-century BCE **Mazarrón 2** wreck in Spain for the open hull and stowage details. At about 8.1 × 2.25 m, it provides a useful scale comparison. Retain the smaller Sprint 1 boat if that works better at the landing. The nearshore boat is a reconstruction inspired by the wreck. [ARQUA wreck and cargo record](https://www.cultura.gob.es/mnarqua/en/colecciones/piezas-seleccionadas/fenicio/barco-2.html), [Mazarrón interpretation centre dimensions](https://www.turismoregiondemurcia.es/en/interpretation_centre/centro-de-interpretacion-del-barco-fenicio-399/)

**Optional offshore merchant ship.** Add this after the landing reads clearly. Use a fuller, higher-sided cargo hull with a rounded middle and gently rising ends. If the silhouette needs rigging, start with one mast, one yard and a small amount of furled fabric. Treat rigging, timber species, and paint as conjectural. Use the Met’s round-hulled merchant imagery for the silhouette and the Ashkelon wrecks for the logic of jar cargo. Avoid warship rams, banks of oars, and later ship features such as a tall sterncastle or ship’s wheel. [Met Museum Phoenician sailing references](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2014/assyria-to-iberia/blog/posts/phoenician-sailing), [Ashkelon wreck survey](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.2307/4126241)

## Blender and browser handoff

- Keep the editable Blender file organized into terrain, water, boats, stations, and props. Check shoreline silhouettes before adding small detail. Use simple collidable ground and reserve fine detail for the visible meshes.
- Deliver the editable scene and one game-ready GLB. The current browser copy at `public/assets/harbor-landing.glb` rotates the direct Blender export 90° about glTF Y and renames `Spawn_Reference` to `SPAWN`. Coordinate with Programmer B before changing that convention. In the current browser scene the camera faces +Z. Under the loader's naming rules, `COL_` marks invisible colliders, `NOCOL_` marks visible non-colliding meshes, and meshes with “water” in their names are non-colliding. [Loader conventions](src/landing/config.ts), [Current export handoff](HANDOFF.md)
- Check the GLB in the browser from the upper path and cargo area. Verify that spawn points toward the landing, the ramp stays walkable, both workshop footprints leave room to pass, and the headland and El-Yasmine read as distinct landforms. A second ship, furnished town, and detailed surf can wait until those checks pass.
